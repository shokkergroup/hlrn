Trevor Haley crossed the Talladega stripe first and appeared to have found the feature win that had escaped him throughout Season 1. HLRN’s review took it away. Race control cited an advance below the yellow line and the contact that followed, disqualified the No. 12, and eventually placed David Applegate first, Brandon Taylor second, and Larkin Boyer third. A later Season 2 companion still identified Haley as the Season 1 champion, leaving the finale with two separate truths: the title survived; the race win did not.

The finale widened the points story before concentrating all attention on the last lap. Boyer, Bryce Hinton, and Nick Bowman divided the three supported stages. A separate sequence saw the No. 12 drop across Shawn Stamper and trigger the race’s big one. That earlier incident belongs to the night’s chronology but should not be merged with the final-lap ruling as if the two were one event.

Haley entered the first final-lap decision with the possibility of closing both the race and the season. Another reset put Kyle Cameron and Wilson at the front for the next launch. When the field finally reached the last lap that mattered, Haley cleared the wreck behind him and crossed first. The primary call and immediate celebration recorded an apparent breakthrough.

That apparent breakthrough fit the season’s unfinished race story. Haley had accumulated stages, podiums, and enough later-supported championship strength to define the year without a feature victory in the accepted ledger. Talladega appeared to supply the missing line at the same moment it closed the schedule. Applegate, Taylor, and Boyer remained relevant because a disqualification would not simply erase the first car; it would move the rest of the accepted order. Boyer had already scored in the stages and stayed close enough to convert the ruling into a podium. The celebration became evidence of what viewers initially saw, not the final result. Haley joined the booth and explained the race while HLRN continued reviewing the closing sequence. The post-race desk returned to the contact and the below-line movement. Race control then disqualified Haley, stating the basis for the decision rather than merely replacing his name on the screen.

The amendment itself required care. The broadcast corrected an initial revised call before reading the accepted podium as Applegate, Taylor, Boyer. For Applegate, the ruling produced a supported victory in only his second season appearance. Taylor and Boyer were elevated with him, and Boyer’s podium completed a night in which he had already earned a stage. Haley lost the feature position, but the later companion’s championship identification means the DQ cannot be silently extended into a claim that he lost the title.

The distinction also closes Haley’s season honestly. His championship case had been built across stages and consistent high finishes, not on a victory that the finale review ultimately removed. The DQ changed the race ledger and the three names attached to Talladega’s podium; the later HLRN championship statement preserved the season ledger separately. Keeping those outcomes apart prevents two equal errors—pretending Haley retained the race win because he won the title, or pretending the race penalty erased a championship the channel still attributes to him.
